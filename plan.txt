  PHASE 5:
  ─────────
  Now ask Can they work together?

  Suppose

    • Coordinate Attention worked well.
    • Boundary Loss worked well.
    • Light Decoder worked well.

  Can we combine them?

  Create another branch.
  Train again.

  Now suppose result is great.
  Now you have your proposed architecture.

  Why don't we combine everything?

  Sometimes improvements conflict.
  Combine only the successful ideas.

══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

  PHASE 6:
  ─────────
  Now let's discuss the ablation study

  Many students misunderstand this.

  An ablation study answers one question:

    Which component of our proposed method actually contributes to the improvement?

  Suppose your final model is:

    Baseline
            │
            ▼
    Coordinate Attention
            │
            ▼
    Light Decoder
            │
            ▼
    Boundary Loss
            │
            ▼
    Ghost Module

  You claim

    "Our architecture is better."

  A reviewer immediately asks:

    Which of these four changes actually made it better?

  So you remove or add one component at a time and measure the effect.

  | Model                          | mIoU     | Params | GFLOPs |
  | ------------------------------ | -------- | ------ | ------ |
  | Baseline                       | 72.1     | 22M    | 60     |
  | Baseline + Attention           | 74.5     | 22.5M  | 62     |
  | Baseline + Decoder             | 74.1     | 23M    | 65     |
  | Baseline + Loss                | 73.2     | 22M    | 60     |
  | Baseline + Efficiency          | 71.9     | 12M    | 35     |
  | Baseline + Attention + Decoder | 75.2     | 23.5M  | 67     |
  | Final (All Compatible Modules) | **75.8** | 18M    | 42     |

  This is exactly what reviewers want: proof that each design decision
  is justified by experiments, not just that the final model happens to work.
