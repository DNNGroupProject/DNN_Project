USGS EarthExplorer: The US Geological Survey offers free access to the entire Landsat archive, providing the historical data necessary for long-term temporal analysis of deforestation.


Curated Environmental Datasets
Hansen Global Forest Change Dataset: Accessible via GEE or the University of Maryland, this dataset maps global tree cover extent, loss, and gain from the year 2000 onwards at a 30-meter resolution. It is highly regarded for training semantic segmentation and change-detection models.

GlobelLand30: A 30-meter resolution global land cover dataset that provides distinct classifications (such as forests, shrublands, and bare lands). These classifications serve as excellent ground-truth labels for supervised DNN training.

SoilGrids: A global digital soil mapping system providing intricate information on soil properties. Because desertification inherently involves soil degradation, integrating this into a multi-modal neural network architecture can significantly improve model accuracy.

Open-Source Tooling

Open-Source Tooling
TATSSI: If you need to generate and preprocess your own custom time-series datasets, the Tools for Analyzing Time Series of Satellite Imagery (TATSSI) is a highly useful open-source Python platform. It automates the downloading, gap-filling, and smoothing of MODIS, VIIRS, Landsat, and Sentinel time-series data specifically for monitoring desertification processes (Tecuapetla-Gómez et al., 2021)
